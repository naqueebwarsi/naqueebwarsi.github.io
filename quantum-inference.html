Research direction 02

Quantum Inference

Statistical prediction and estimation for quantum systems, with emphasis on unbiased procedures, nonlinear functionals, and fundamental efficiency limits.

Research accomplished

Estimation, prediction, and efficiency

My work in quantum inference studies how to estimate properties of unknown quantum states from measurement data. One direction develops a framework for unbiased estimation of polynomial functionals of quantum states through permutation-invariant quantum U-statistics. It characterizes the estimator and its variance, identifying the leading contribution through the functional gradient and connecting the analysis to quantum Cramér–Rao theory and symmetric logarithmic derivatives.

A complementary direction studies Bayesian prediction of unknown pure quantum states under relative-entropy loss. The associated work establishes a reduction from general measurement effects to a more tractable class and proves global optimality of the prediction rule. Together, these projects connect quantum information theory with mathematical statistics, Bayesian decision theory, and quantum estimation.
